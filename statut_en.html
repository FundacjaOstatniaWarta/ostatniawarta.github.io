STATUTE OF THE "OSTATNIA WARTA" FOUNDATION

Chapter I. General Provisions

§ 1

The Foundation under the name "Ostatnia Warta" Foundation, hereinafter referred to as the Foundation, established by Przemysław Belchnerowski, hereinafter referred to as the Founder, by a notarial deed drawn up on 11.02.2026, before the notary Karol Aleksandrowicz, Repertory A No. 926/2026, operates under the provisions of Polish law and this Statute.

§ 2

In its activities, the Foundation is guided by the respect for the inherent and inalienable dignity of a human being, solidarity, and universal humanistic values, and for believers also by the values stemming from the Christian ethos of mercy, providing help to every human being regardless of their religion or worldview.

§ 3

The Foundation's motto is: "Stay here and keep watch with me!" (Gospel according to St. Matthew 26, 38).

§ 4

  1. The seat of the Foundation is the city of Gdańsk.
  2. The area of the Foundation's operations is the territory of the Republic of Poland. To properly achieve its goals, the Foundation may also conduct activities outside the borders of the Republic of Poland.
  3. The duration of the Foundation is indefinite.
  4. The Foundation may use a distinctive graphic symbol and equivalents of its name in foreign languages.
  5. The State Treasury, local government units, state or local government legal entities do not have control over the Foundation within the meaning of Art. 4 point 4 of the Act of February 16, 2007, on competition and consumer protection.

§ 5

The Foundation is supervised by the Minister responsible for Health.

Chapter II. Objectives and Principles of Operations

§ 6

The goal of the Foundation is comprehensive health protection and social assistance, in particular:

  1. Providing comprehensive assistance to chronically ill, terminally ill, and dying people, in particular by ensuring accompaniment and presence in the last moments of life.
  2. Activities aimed at improving the quality of life, mental well-being, and respecting the dignity of terminally ill people.
  3. Counteracting the loneliness of dying people in medical and care facilities, as well as in private homes.
  4. Supporting families and caregivers of terminally ill people in the care process and during the mourning period.
  5. Promoting the idea of accompanying the dying ("sitting service") and familiarizing the topic of death.
  6. Social education in the field of palliative care and thanatology.
  7. Uniting people of good will ready to help those in need.
  8. Activities for people with disabilities and severely and chronically ill people (including oncological patients), including support in social rehabilitation and the organization of therapeutic, workshop, and activation classes.
  9. Supporting the activity, independence, and social integration of the elderly, including those struggling with old age diseases and dementia.
  10. Conducting educational and preventive activities in the field of public health, including the prevention of addictions, as well as infectious (incl. HIV/AIDS) and incurable diseases.
  11. Financial and in-kind support for people in particularly difficult life, health, or material situations.
  12. Activities for the social and professional reintegration of people at risk of social exclusion, in particular through the creation of jobs within a Social Enterprise.

§ 7

  1. The Foundation achieves its statutory goals by conducting unpaid public benefit activity and paid public benefit activity.
  2. Revenues from paid public benefit activity serve exclusively the implementation of statutory goals.
  3. The Foundation's activities are exclusively supportive, social, and educational in nature. The Foundation is not a medical entity within the meaning of the provisions on medical activity, does not provide health services, does not make medical diagnoses, and does not make therapeutic decisions.
  4. The Foundation's activities complement, rather than replace, professional medical care.

§ 7a

The subject of unpaid public benefit activity is:

  1. Organizing and coordinating free care volunteering in hospitals, hospices, nursing homes, and private homes.
  2. Providing in-kind and financial assistance to patients, including in particular financing or co-financing the costs of treatment (including foreign), medical procedures, rehabilitation, purchase of medicines, hygiene products, medical equipment, and specialized transport, as well as financial support in meeting the basic living needs of patients.
  3. Organizing and financing travel and commuting costs for volunteers and staff to the places where patients stay, including in the form of reimbursement for the use of private vehicles (mileage allowance).
  4. Organizing free social campaigns, information campaigns, public fundraisers, and charity events.
  5. Cooperating with public administration bodies and other entities through the implementation of social projects and participation in open competitions for the implementation of public tasks (grants).
  6. Organizing places for performing socially useful work for people directed by the justice system as part of their social reintegration process.
  7. Supporting the activities of other people and institutions with convergent goals.

§ 7b

The subject of paid public benefit activity is:

  1. Organizing and coordinating care volunteering and respite care in the form of accompaniment (Sitting Service) and care of people accompanying the dying process (End-of-life Doula).
  2. Conducting specialized training and workshops, including in particular training for End-of-life Doulas and in the field of palliative care and thanatology.
  3. Running a rental of medical and rehabilitation equipment, and devices facilitating care for the sick.
  4. Implementing paid public tasks commissioned by state and local government administration bodies and cooperating with medical and business entities in the scope of the Foundation's goals.
  5. Organizing and conducting specialized training as well as preventive and educational programs, including in the field of addiction prevention and safety.

Chapter III. Assets and Revenues

§ 8

The assets of the Foundation consist of the founding fund in the amount of 1000 PLN (in words: one thousand zlotys) and other property acquired by the Foundation in the course of its operations.

§ 9

  1. The Foundation's revenues may come from:
    • donations, inheritances, bequests,
    • subsidies, subventions, and grants (domestic and foreign),
    • public fundraisers and charity events,
    • movable and immovable property of the Foundation,
    • interest and bank deposits,
    • paid public benefit activity,
    • dividends and profits from shares in commercial law companies.
  2. In order to multiply its assets, the Foundation may create commercial law companies, join them, and acquire shares or stocks in them.
  3. The Foundation does not conduct business activity.
  4. All of the Foundation's income is allocated to its statutory activities.
  5. The excess of revenues over costs (profit/balance surplus) obtained from the conducted activity cannot be distributed among the Founder, members of the authorities, and persons employed in the Foundation, but must be allocated entirely to the implementation of statutory goals (public benefit activity).
  6. In the event of granting the Foundation the status of a Social Enterprise, at least 30% of the excess of revenues over costs is allocated to the social and professional reintegration fund for employees.

§ 10

The Foundation covers all costs necessary for the implementation of statutory goals and the current organizational and administrative functioning of the Foundation.

§ 11

It is forbidden to:

  1. Grant loans or secure obligations with the Foundation's assets in relation to the Founder, foundation authorities, or employees, as well as people close to them.
  2. Transfer the Foundation's assets to the Founder, foundation authorities, or employees, as well as their close ones, on terms other than in relation to third parties.
  3. Use the assets for the benefit of the Founder, foundation authorities, or employees, as well as their close ones, on terms other than in relation to third parties, unless this use directly results from the statutory goal.
  4. Purchase goods or services from legal entities organizationally affiliated with the Foundation or entities in which the persons mentioned in § 12 of this statute participate, on terms other than in the case of third parties or at higher than market prices.

Chapter IV. Authorities of the Foundation

§ 12

The authorities of the Foundation are:

  • Foundation Council.
  • Management Board of the Foundation.
  • Honorary Council (as an advisory body).
  • Employees' Meeting.

A. FOUNDATION COUNCIL

§ 13

  1. The Foundation Council is the constituting, controlling, and opinion-forming body of the Foundation.
  2. The Foundation Council consists of 2 to 7 members.
  3. Members of the first and subsequent compositions of the Council are appointed and dismissed by the Founder. In the event of the Founder's death, subsequent members of the Council are appointed by the decision of the Foundation Council.
  4. The term of office of the Foundation Council is 5 years.
  5. Membership in the Council ceases as a result of: death, written resignation, or dismissal by an authorized body.
  6. Each member of the Foundation Council may be re-elected to the Foundation Council, unless this would violate applicable law.

§ 14

The competences of the Foundation Council include:

  1. Setting the main directions of the Foundation's activities.
  2. Supervising the activities of the Management Board.
  3. Reviewing and approving the annual reports of the Management Board (financial and substantive) and granting discharge to the Management Board.
  4. Appointing and dismissing members of the Management Board (subject to § 16 sec. 3).
  5. Appointing by resolution an external Plenipotentiary of the Supervisory Authority for Employee Affairs, authorized to independently represent the Foundation in employment contracts and civil law contracts with members of the Management Board and the Foundation Council.

§ 15

  1. The Foundation Council elects the Chairman of the Council from among its members.
  2. The Foundation Council meets at least once a year.
  3. Resolutions of the Council are passed by a simple majority of votes. In the event of an equal number of votes, the Chairman's vote is decisive.
  4. Meetings of the Council and voting may take place using means of remote electronic communication.
  5. Members of the Foundation Council may perform their functions socially or for remuneration determined by the Founder based on civil law contracts or employment contracts.

B. MANAGEMENT BOARD

§ 16

  1. The Management Board of the Foundation manages the Foundation's activities and represents it externally.
  2. The Management Board consists of 1 to 3 people, including the President.
  3. The first composition of the Management Board (including the President) is appointed by the Founder. Subsequent compositions of the Management Board are appointed by the Foundation Council.
  4. The term of office of the Management Board is 5 years. Members of the Management Board may be appointed for subsequent terms multiple times.
  5. Members of the Management Board may perform their functions socially or for remuneration.
  6. The President of the Foundation's Management Board is the manager of the Workplace for the Foundation's employees within the meaning of labor law provisions.

§ 17

The competences of the Foundation's Management Board include making decisions on all matters not reserved for the competences of other bodies, and in particular:

  1. Managing the current activities of the Foundation and representing it externally.
  2. Managing the Foundation's assets and directing its financial economy.
  3. Deciding on the acquisition, disposal, and encumbrance of real estate and other fixed assets.
  4. Incurring property liabilities, including loans, borrowings, and leases.
  5. Accepting donations, inheritances, and bequests.
  6. Employing employees and determining the amount of their remuneration.
  7. Administrative and technical service of the Foundation Council.

§ 18

Each member of the Management Board is authorized independently to submit declarations of will on behalf of the Foundation, including in property matters.

C. HONORARY COUNCIL

§ 19

  1. The Management Board of the Foundation may appoint to the Honorary Council natural and legal persons particularly meritorious for the goals of the Foundation or social life.
  2. The Honorary Council is an advisory body. Membership in it is an honorary function.

D. EMPLOYEES' MEETING

§ 19a

  1. The Employees' Meeting is an advisory and consultative body of the Foundation, consisting of all its employees.
  2. The competences of the Employees' Meeting include in particular:
    • getting acquainted no less than once a year with the results of the Foundation's activities and expressing opinions in this regard;
    • expressing opinions on work regulations or rules for organizing work and remuneration set by the Foundation and proposed changes in this regard;
    • expressing opinions on the Foundation's planned activities for the social and professional reintegration of employees.

Chapter V. Amendment to the Statute and Liquidation

§ 20

  1. Amendments to the Statute, including changing or expanding the goals of the Foundation, are made by the Founder by means of a declaration of will in the form of a resolution.
  2. After the Founder's death, this right passes to the Foundation Council, which adopts a resolution by a 2/3 majority of votes, unless the Founder indicated by resolution another natural person as his successor - in this case, all of the Founder's rights pass to the indicated person.

§ 21

  1. The Foundation is liquidated if the goals for which it was established are achieved, or if its financial resources and assets are exhausted.
  2. The decision to liquidate the Foundation is made by the Founder.
  3. The Liquidator is appointed by the Founder (or the Management Board, if the Founder so decides).
  4. The Foundation's assets remaining after the liquidation of the Social Enterprise must be transferred to another Social Enterprise operating in the region or to the Labor Fund.

§ 22

Succession of the Founder's rights:

  1. The status of the Founder as the historical creator of the Foundation is an unchangeable, personal, and inalienable state.
  2. The Founder may, by way of a resolution, indicate a natural person who will take over all his rights and obligations provided for in this Statute in the event of his death, permanent loss of legal capacity, or other permanent inability to exercise these rights.
  3. A permanent inability to exercise the rights referred to in sec. 2 shall be deemed in particular to be the submission by the Founder to the Foundation's Management Board of a written declaration of permanent and final refraining from exercising all statutory rights. The submission of such a declaration constitutes solely a resignation from authoritative competences and in no way nullifies the historical status of the Founder referred to in sec. 1.
  4. In the absence of an indication of the person referred to in sec. 2, after the Founder's death, all his rights and obligations pass to his statutory heirs. If there are several of them, they exercise the Founder's rights jointly, through a plenipotentiary appointed from among them.

Chapter VI. Final Provisions

§ 23

  1. The Foundation uses a header stamp, including a round red stamp, containing the emblem, name, and seat of the Foundation, and may also use a distinctive graphic symbol (logo).
  2. The Foundation may establish badges, honorary medals, and award them along with other prizes and distinctions to natural and legal persons of merit to the Foundation.
  3. In matters not regulated by this Statute, the provisions of the Civil Code, the Act on Foundations, the Act on Social Economy, and the Act on Public Benefit Activity and Volunteerism shall apply.
  4. The Statute in the wording given by this uniform text enters into force on the day the amendments are entered into the National Court Register.
