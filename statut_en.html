STATUTE OF THE “OSTATNIA WARTA” FOUNDATION

Consolidated text adopted by Founder’s Resolution No. 8/2026 of 23 November 2026.
Entered in the National Court Register (KRS) on 26 November 2026 (KRS 0001224367).
This English version is a translation provided for information only. The Polish text is legally binding.

Chapter I. General Provisions

§ 1

The Foundation named Fundacja “Ostatnia Warta”, hereinafter referred to as the Foundation, established by Przemysław Belchnerowski, hereinafter referred to as the Founder, by a notarial deed drawn up on 11 February 2026 before notary Karol Aleksandrowicz, Repertory A No. 926/2026, operates pursuant to Polish law, in particular the Act of 6 April 1984 on Foundations and the Act of 24 April 2003 on Public Benefit Activity and Volunteerism, as well as this Statute.

§ 2

In its activities, the Foundation is guided by respect for the inherent and inalienable dignity of the human person, by solidarity and by universal humanistic values, and – for believers – also by values stemming from the Christian ethos of mercy, providing help to every person regardless of their religion or worldview.

§ 3

The Foundation’s motto is the words: “Stay here and keep watch with me!” (Gospel according to St. Matthew 26:38).

§ 4

  1. The seat of the Foundation is the city of Gdańsk.
  2. The Foundation operates within the territory of the Republic of Poland. To properly achieve its goals, the Foundation may also operate outside the Republic of Poland.
  3. The Foundation is established for an indefinite period.
  4. The Foundation may use a distinguishing graphic symbol and equivalents of its name in foreign languages.

§ 5

Supervision over the Foundation is exercised by the minister competent for health.

Chapter II. Goals and Operating Principles of the Foundation

§ 6

The goal of the Foundation is comprehensive health protection and social assistance, in particular:

  1. Providing comprehensive assistance to chronically ill, terminally ill and dying persons, in particular by ensuring that they are accompanied and not alone in the last moments of their lives.
  2. Activities to improve the quality of life, mental well-being and respect for the dignity of terminally ill persons.
  3. Counteracting the loneliness of dying persons in medical and care facilities and in private homes.
  4. Supporting families and carers of terminally ill persons during care and in bereavement.
  5. Promoting the idea of accompanying the dying (“sitting service”) and making the subject of death less of a taboo.
  6. Public education in palliative care and thanatology.
  7. Bringing together people of good will who are ready to help those in need.
  8. Activities for persons with disabilities and for severely and chronically ill persons (including cancer patients), comprising support in social rehabilitation and the organisation of therapeutic, workshop and activating activities.
  9. Supporting the activity, independence and social integration of older people, including those living with age-related conditions and dementia.
  10. Educational and preventive activities in public health, including the prevention of addictions and of infectious (including HIV/AIDS) and incurable diseases.
  11. Financial and material support for persons in a particularly difficult life, health or financial situation.
  12. Activities for the social integration of persons at risk of social exclusion, in particular lonely, older and dependent persons.
  13. Supporting family carers of chronically ill, dependent and dying persons, including through respite care.
  14. Education and skills development for family carers, volunteers and persons accompanying the sick, the elderly and the dying.
  15. Counteracting the exclusion of sick and older persons living in remote, small or poorly connected localities.
  16. Promoting and organising volunteering, and charitable activity.

§ 7

  1. The Foundation pursues its goals by conducting unpaid and paid public benefit activities within the meaning of the Act of 24 April 2003 on Public Benefit Activity and Volunteerism.
  2. The Foundation does not conduct business activity.
  3. The Foundation’s activities are exclusively supportive, social and educational in nature. The Foundation is not a medical entity within the meaning of the provisions on medical activity, does not provide health services, does not make medical diagnoses and does not make therapeutic decisions.
  4. The Foundation’s activities complement, and do not replace, professional medical care.
  5. The Foundation pursues its goals in particular with the participation of volunteers. The Foundation may employ staff and conclude civil law contracts.
  6. The Foundation may pursue its goals in cooperation with public administration bodies, medical entities, care facilities, non-governmental organisations and other entities.

§ 8

The unpaid public benefit activities of the Foundation comprise:

  1. Organising and providing free accompaniment for dying, terminally ill and severely ill persons (“Sitting Service”) in hospitals, hospices, care homes and private homes, including in the form of volunteer shifts and patrols.
  2. Free respite care in the form of accompaniment, giving relief to families and carers of sick, dependent and dying persons.
  3. Recruiting, preparing and coordinating volunteers, including free training for the Foundation’s volunteers.
  4. Providing material and financial assistance to beneficiaries, in particular financing or co-financing the costs of treatment (including treatment abroad), medical procedures, rehabilitation, medicines, hygiene products, medical equipment and specialised transport, as well as financial support in meeting the basic living needs of beneficiaries.
  5. Organising and financing the travel of volunteers and staff to the places where beneficiaries stay, including to remote and hard-to-reach localities, also by reimbursing the costs of using private vehicles (mileage allowance) and by maintaining the Foundation’s vehicles.
  6. Free lending of medical and rehabilitation equipment and of devices that make caring for a sick person easier.
  7. Supporting families and carers during care and in bereavement, including meetings and support groups.
  8. Free educational and information activities, including meetings, lectures, workshops, social campaigns and publications promoting the idea of accompanying the dying.
  9. Organising public collections, social campaigns and charity events.
  10. Carrying out public tasks commissioned by public administration bodies, including through open calls for proposals, and taking part in grant programmes.
  11. Organising placements for community service for persons referred by the justice system as part of their social reintegration.
  12. Supporting the activities of other persons and institutions with similar goals.

§ 9

  1. The paid public benefit activities of the Foundation comprise:
    1. Organising and running training courses, courses and workshops, in person and online, in particular: for families and carers on accompanying a dying person; on supporting older, chronically ill and dependent persons; preparing persons who accompany the dying (end-of-life doulas); and on palliative care, thanatology and bereavement support.
    2. Publishing and making available, including selling, the Foundation’s own publications and educational materials, in print and electronic form, related to the Foundation’s goals.
    3. Selling donated items, including through an online shop, stalls at events and charity auctions.
    4. Accompaniment and respite care provided on behalf of a third party, in particular a local government unit, a social welfare centre or a care or medical facility, which pays the Foundation a fee.
  2. Fees for paid public benefit activities are set by the Management Board on the basis of a cost calculation, so that the remuneration for an activity of a given type does not exceed the costs of that activity.
  3. The same activities may be conducted free of charge or for a fee, in particular training that is free for the Foundation’s volunteers and paid for other persons. The Foundation keeps separate accounting records for unpaid and paid public benefit activities to the extent that allows the revenues, costs and results of each to be determined.
  4. Revenue from paid public benefit activities serves exclusively to conduct public benefit activities.

Chapter III. Assets and Revenues of the Foundation

§ 10

The Foundation’s assets consist of the founding fund of PLN 1,000 (one thousand zlotys) and other property acquired by the Foundation in the course of its activities.

§ 11

  1. The Foundation’s revenues include in particular:
    1. cash and in-kind donations, inheritances and bequests;
    2. subsidies, subventions and grants, domestic and foreign, from public and private sources;
    3. funds from public collections, online fundraising and charity events;
    4. revenue from paid public benefit activities;
    5. exemplary damages (nawiązki) and monetary payments awarded to the Foundation by courts;
    6. interest on funds held in bank accounts and deposits.
  2. All revenue of the Foundation is used to pursue its statutory goals and to cover the Foundation’s operating costs, including organisational and administrative costs.
  3. Any surplus of revenues over costs may not be distributed among the Founder, members of the Foundation’s bodies, employees or persons close to them, and is used in full for public benefit activities.
  4. Funds from donations, subsidies, grants and collections earmarked for a specific purpose are spent in accordance with that purpose.

§ 12

  1. It is prohibited to:
    1. grant loans or secure liabilities with the Foundation’s assets in favour of the Founder, members of the Foundation’s bodies or employees, or persons with whom they are married, cohabiting, related by blood or affinity in the direct line, related by blood or affinity in the collateral line up to the second degree, or connected by adoption, guardianship or curatorship, hereinafter referred to as “close persons”;
    2. transfer the Foundation’s assets to the persons listed in letter a on terms other than those applicable to third parties, in particular free of charge or on preferential terms;
    3. use the Foundation’s assets for the benefit of the persons listed in letter a on terms other than those applicable to third parties, unless such use follows directly from a statutory goal;
    4. purchase goods or services from entities in which the persons listed in letter a participate, on terms other than those applicable to third parties or at prices higher than market prices.
  2. The prohibitions referred to in section 1 do not apply to remuneration for work or services or to reimbursement of documented costs, including volunteers’ travel costs, paid on the same terms as to third parties.

Chapter IV. Bodies of the Foundation

§ 13

  1. The bodies of the Foundation are:
    1. the Foundation Council;
    2. the Management Board of the Foundation.
  2. An Honorary Council may operate alongside the Foundation as an advisory body, as referred to in § 21.

A. THE FOUNDATION COUNCIL

§ 14

  1. The Foundation Council is the Foundation’s supervisory and control body and its advisory body.
  2. The Foundation Council consists of 2 to 7 members.
  3. Members of the Council are appointed and dismissed by the Founder. In the cases referred to in § 23 sections 2–4, this right is exercised by the Founder’s successor or, in the absence of a successor, by the Foundation Council, which supplements its composition by a resolution adopted by a 2/3 majority of votes.
  4. A member of the Foundation Council may not at the same time be a member of the Management Board.
  5. The term of office of the Foundation Council is 5 years. A Council member appointed during a term of office serves until the end of that term.
  6. Membership of the Council ends upon death, written resignation, dismissal by the authorised body or expiry of the term of office.
  7. A Council member may be appointed for further terms.

§ 15

The competences of the Foundation Council include:

  1. Setting the main directions of the Foundation’s activities.
  2. Supervising and controlling the activities of the Management Board, including the right to inspect all of the Foundation’s documents and to request explanations from the Management Board.
  3. Reviewing and approving the Management Board’s annual reports (financial and narrative) and granting discharge to members of the Management Board.
  4. Acting as the approving body within the meaning of the accounting regulations, including deciding whether to keep, or to stop keeping, simplified records of revenues and costs.
  5. Appointing and dismissing members of the Management Board, subject to § 17 section 3.
  6. Setting the rules and amount of remuneration of members of the Management Board.
  7. Representing the Foundation in contracts and disputes with members of the Management Board, on the terms set out in § 20.
  8. Giving opinions on matters submitted by the Founder or the Management Board.

§ 16

  1. The Foundation Council elects a Chair of the Council from among its members.
  2. The Foundation Council meets at least once a year. Meetings are convened by the Chair on their own initiative or at the request of the Founder or the Management Board.
  3. Resolutions of the Council are adopted by a simple majority of votes in the presence of at least half of its members. In the event of a tie, the Chair has the casting vote.
  4. Council meetings and votes may take place using means of electronic communication at a distance. Resolutions may also be adopted in writing (by circulation), provided that all members of the Council have been notified of the draft resolution.
  5. Members of the Foundation Council perform their functions on a voluntary basis or for remuneration for their appointment, in an amount set by the Founder. Council members may be reimbursed for justified costs related to their work on the Council.

B. THE MANAGEMENT BOARD

§ 17

  1. The Management Board directs the Foundation’s activities and represents it externally.
  2. The Management Board consists of 1 to 3 persons, including the President.
  3. The first Management Board, including the President, is appointed by the Founder. Subsequent members of the Management Board are appointed and dismissed by the Foundation Council.
  4. The term of office of the Management Board is 5 years. Members of the Management Board may be reappointed for further terms any number of times.
  5. Membership of the Management Board ends upon death, written resignation, dismissal or expiry of the term of office.
  6. The Founder may be a member of the Management Board.
  7. Members of the Management Board may perform their functions on a voluntary basis or for remuneration, including under an employment contract or a civil law contract. The rules and amount of remuneration are set by the Foundation Council by resolution.

§ 18

  1. The Management Board decides on all matters not reserved to the Founder or the Foundation Council, in particular:
    1. directing the Foundation’s day-to-day activities and representing it externally;
    2. managing the Foundation’s assets and its finances;
    3. deciding on the acquisition, disposal and encumbrance of real estate and other fixed assets;
    4. incurring financial liabilities, including credits, loans and leases;
    5. accepting donations, inheritances and bequests;
    6. setting fees for paid public benefit activities in accordance with § 9 section 2;
    7. employing staff, concluding civil law contracts and agreements with volunteers, and setting remuneration, subject to § 15 point 6 and § 20;
    8. preparing annual reports on the Foundation’s activities and submitting them to the Foundation Council;
    9. providing administrative and technical support to the Foundation Council.
  2. Where the Management Board has more than one member, its resolutions are adopted by a simple majority of votes. In the event of a tie, the President has the casting vote.

§ 19

  1. Each member of the Management Board is authorised to make declarations of will on behalf of the Foundation independently, including in property matters, subject to § 20.
  2. Actions under labour law with respect to the Foundation’s employees are performed by the President of the Management Board or, in the President’s absence, by another member of the Management Board, subject to § 20.

§ 20

  1. In contracts, including employment contracts and civil law contracts, and in disputes between the Foundation and a member of the Management Board, the Foundation is represented by the Foundation Council, on whose behalf the Chair of the Council or another Council member designated in a Council resolution acts, or by a proxy appointed by a resolution of the Foundation Council.
  2. The proxy referred to in section 1 may be a person who is not a member of the Foundation’s bodies. The resolution appointing the proxy specifies the scope of the authorisation.
  3. The person or body referred to in section 1 also performs actions under labour law with respect to a member of the Management Board employed by the Foundation.
  4. Sections 1–3 do not apply to contracts from which the Foundation obtains a benefit only, in particular donations to the Foundation. In such contracts the Foundation is represented by the Management Board in accordance with § 19 section 1.

C. THE HONORARY COUNCIL

§ 21

  1. The Management Board may appoint to the Honorary Council natural and legal persons who have rendered particular service to the Foundation’s goals or to public life.
  2. The Honorary Council is an advisory body and is not a body of the Foundation. Membership of it is an honorary function.

Chapter V. Rights of the Founder, Amendment of the Statute and Liquidation

§ 22

  1. The Founder holds the rights set out in the Statute, in particular appointing and dismissing members of the Foundation Council, setting their remuneration, amending the Statute and deciding on the liquidation of the Foundation.
  2. The Founder exercises these rights by written resolutions, including in electronic form.

§ 23

Succession of the Founder’s rights:

  1. The Founder’s status as the historical creator of the Foundation is permanent, personal and non-transferable.
  2. The Founder may, by resolution, designate a natural person who will assume all of the Founder’s rights and obligations under this Statute in the event of the Founder’s death, permanent loss of legal capacity or other permanent inability to exercise those rights.
  3. Permanent inability to exercise the rights referred to in section 2 includes in particular the Founder’s submission to the Foundation Council of a written declaration of permanent and final abstention from exercising any statutory rights. Such a declaration constitutes solely a resignation from decision-making powers and in no way affects the Founder’s historical status referred to in section 1.
  4. If no person has been designated under section 2, or if that person cannot or does not wish to exercise the Founder’s rights, all of the Founder’s rights and obligations are exercised by the Foundation Council, adopting resolutions by a 2/3 majority of votes.

§ 24

  1. Amendments to the Statute, including changes to or extensions of the Foundation’s goals, are made by the Founder by resolution.
  2. In the cases referred to in § 23 sections 2–4, the right to amend the Statute is exercised by the Founder’s successor or, in the absence of a successor, by the Foundation Council by a 2/3 majority of votes.

§ 25

  1. The Foundation is liquidated when the goals for which it was established have been achieved or when its financial resources and assets have been exhausted.
  2. The decision to liquidate the Foundation is made by the Founder or, in the cases referred to in § 23 sections 2–4, by the Founder’s successor or by the Foundation Council by a 2/3 majority of votes.
  3. The liquidator is the Management Board, unless the liquidation resolution appoints another person.
  4. Assets remaining after the liquidation of the Foundation are allocated to a non-governmental organisation operating in Poland with goals similar to those of the Foundation, indicated in the liquidation resolution. These assets may not be transferred to the Founder, members of the Foundation’s bodies, employees or persons close to them.

Chapter VI. Final Provisions

§ 26

  1. The Foundation may use a seal bearing its name, its seat and its graphic symbol (logo).
  2. The Foundation may establish badges and honorary medals and award them, together with other prizes and distinctions, to natural and legal persons who have rendered service to the Foundation.
  3. Matters not governed by this Statute are subject to the Act on Foundations, the Act on Public Benefit Activity and Volunteerism and the Civil Code.
  4. Members of the Foundation’s bodies appointed before this consolidated text enters into force continue to serve until the end of the term for which they were appointed. A proxy appointed by the Foundation Council under the previous provisions of the Statute to represent the Foundation in contracts with members of the Management Board and the Council retains their authorisation within the scope referred to in § 20.
  5. The Statute as set out in this consolidated text enters into force on the date the changes are entered in the National Court Register.
